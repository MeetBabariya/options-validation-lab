Independent validation · Author: Meet Babariya · Generated automatically 25 Oct 2026, 12:59 UTC · Code and data: github.com/MeetBabariya/options-validation-lab

1. Executive summary

Seven models were validated for marking and hedging BTC options: SVI, SSVI, SABR, Heston, Bates, Black-Scholes and an adaptive challenger delta. SVI and SABR are fit for marking: they price 99% and 92% of quotes inside the bid-ask spread. Heston is not approved: it needs implausible dynamics, misprices short-dated options and hedges worse than Black-Scholes. For hedging, no fixed smile-dynamics assumption beats the plain sticky-strike delta over a year, because the spot-volatility correlation changes sign between months (-0.36 to +0.60).

2. Scope and intended use

The models are assessed for two uses: marking (producing an implied-volatility surface consistent with the market and free of static arbitrage) and hedging (producing a delta that minimises the one-day hedging error). Out of scope: exotic pricing, vega hedging, transaction costs and capital.

9. Findings register

IDSeverityAreaFindingRecommendation
F1HighHestonFitting BTC smiles requires implausible dynamics (median kappa 47, vol of vol 8.0) and still misprices short-dated options; parameters are tightly identified (bootstrap CV 3% or less), so this is model misspecification, not noise.Do not use. A jump extension (Bates) was tested, see F8.
F2HighSmile-dynamics deltasSpot-vol correlation changes sign between months (-0.36 to +0.60). Every fixed assumption wins in one regime and loses in the other.Keep sticky-strike as default; evaluate regime-adaptive deltas.
F3MediumSticky moneyness, HestonBoth imply that IV rises when BTC rises; over the year it fell. Hedging error is 12% and 15% worse than BS, intervals exclude zero.Do not use sticky-moneyness deltas for BTC.
F4MediumAll hedgesHedging errors are heavy-tailed: the worst 1% of option-days carry about 45% of the squared error.Report robust metrics alongside SSE; stress-test on large-move days.
F5MediumSSVIOne global skew parameter cannot match the short-dated BTC put skew (1 to 2 vol pts).Use per-expiry SVI or SABR for short maturities.
F6LowSVI implementationVery short expiries produced degenerate fits (negative total variance, extreme deltas) until optimiser starts were scaled to the slice and total variance was bounded at zero. Fixed and covered by tests.Keep the regression tests; alert on any |delta| > 1.
F8HighBatesAdding jumps cuts the median IV error from 1.27 to 0.78 vol pts, but only by pricing a rare, very large crash (0.55 per year, mean jump -9%). The jump parameters trade off along a ridge (|correlation| 1.00 in the bootstrap); only combinations such as the jump variance (0.091 per year, CV 15%) are pinned down. The diffusion stays implausible (kappa 23). The objective has a second, much worse basin with frequent small jumps, and over the year the daily fit switches between the two: jump rate below 0.5 per year on 42% of days, at the 60 bound on 21%. Hedge: -15.0% vs BS (-26.5 to -6.7%), no better than Heston.Not for hedging. Calibrate from several starts in both basins; report jump risk as a combination, not separate parameters.
F7LowDataBacktest uses Deribit marks, not executable quotes, with daily rebalancing and no costs; only options trading on both days are included.Repeat with order-book snapshots when available.

10. Limitations

Marks are Deribit model prices, not executable quotes. Hedging is daily with no transaction costs. Option and forward are measured at each instrument's last trade in the window, and only options that trade on both days are included, which favours liquid strikes. Results cover BTC only.
